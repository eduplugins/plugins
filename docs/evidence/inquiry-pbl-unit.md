# PBL Unit Creator — Evidence Base

**Skill file:** `plugins/skills/inquiry/pbl-unit/SKILL.md`
**Category:** Inquiry

## What the skill does
Builds a complete project-based learning unit anchored in a real-world driving question, structured through the same five/six-phase inquiry cycle as the IBL skill (Tuning In, Finding Out, Sorting Out, Going Further, Drawing Conclusions and Sharing, optional Taking Action), but organised specifically around producing a genuine public product for a real audience, with curriculum links mapped throughout rather than added at the end.

## Evidence base

### Gold Standard PBL: Essential Project Design Elements
- **Publisher/author:** Buck Institute for Education / PBLWorks
- **Year:** 2019 (model in current use)
- **URL:** https://www.pblworks.org/what-is-pbl/gold-standard-project-design
- **Australian source:** No — US-based, but this is the most widely referenced PBL design framework internationally, including in Australian PBL practice
- **What it says:** Defines seven essential elements that distinguish rigorous PBL from "dessert projects": a challenging problem/question, sustained inquiry, authenticity, student voice & choice, reflection, critique & revision, and a public product shared with an audience beyond the classroom.
- **Relevance to this skill:** This is essentially the design specification the skill's own structure follows — driving question (challenging question), the five/six-phase cycle (sustained inquiry), real-world context and audience (authenticity, public product), and student agency levels in Step 1 (voice & choice). The skill's description explicitly frames PBL as requiring "a genuine public product at the end," which is Gold Standard PBL's own defining distinction from ordinary project work.

### Project-Based Learning: A Literature Review
- **Publisher/author:** Barbara Condliffe, with Janet Quint, Mary G. Visher, Michael R. Bangser, Sonia Drohojowska, Larissa Saco, Elizabeth Nelson (MDRC)
- **Year:** 2017
- **URL:** https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED578933
- **Australian source:** No
- **What it says:** Reviewing the K-12 PBL evidence base, the report concludes PBL's effectiveness is "promising but not proven" — some positive effects appear in science and social studies, evidence is more limited in maths and literacy, and many studies suffer from weak research designs and inadequate measures of deeper learning, while implementation is demanding for teachers (classroom management, role shifts, professional development needs).
- **Relevance to this skill:** A direct, evidence-honest counterweight to treating PBL as self-evidently effective — it's especially relevant to the skill's "Finding Out" and "Going Further" phases (Step 3), where the literature review's implementation-difficulty findings (teacher role shift, classroom management demands) suggest these phases need genuinely strong scaffolding and milestones, not just good intentions, particularly outside science/humanities subjects.

### Visible Learning MetaX — Problem-based learning
- **Publisher/author:** Corwin / John Hattie (Hattie holds a professorial position at the University of Melbourne)
- **Year:** Ongoing meta-analytic database; effect size figures current as of 2026 access
- **URL:** https://www.visiblelearningmetax.com/influences/view/problem-based_learning
- **Australian source:** No (international database), but its lead researcher is Melbourne-based and it is widely cited in Australian school evidence policy
- **What it says:** Across 31 meta-analyses (1,059 studies, ~103,700 students), problem-based learning — where students in groups decide what they need to learn to resolve a problem — has a weighted mean effect size of 0.53, above Hattie's 0.40 "hinge point," rated as having "potential to accelerate" achievement.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Provides quantitative support that project/problem-based approaches can be effective — but the effect size describes PBL broadly, not specifically PBL with all seven Gold Standard elements or PBL with a public product, so it should be read as supportive-in-general rather than validating this skill's specific implementation choices.

### Explicit instruction (explainer)
- **Publisher/author:** AERO (Australian Education Research Organisation)
- **Year:** September 2023
- **URL:** https://www.edresearch.edu.au/summaries-explainers/explainers/explicit-instruction
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Drawing on cognitive load theory and Kirschner, Sweller & Clark (2006), AERO argues guided/explicit instruction outperforms minimally guided approaches for novices building foundational knowledge, with inquiry-style application most effective once that foundation is in place.
- **Relevance to this skill:** The most relevant Australian evidence-body caution for this skill's "Finding Out" phase (Step 3) — where students "gather information, develop knowledge, and build skills" needed to answer the driving question — AERO's position implies this knowledge-building stage benefits from more explicit teacher-led instruction than the current phase description ("research task / investigation / expert input") implies on its own.

## How this evidence should sharpen the skill
1. **Make "Finding Out" (Phase 2) explicitly a mix of explicit instruction and investigation, not investigation alone.** Currently this phase lists activities as "research task / investigation / expert input / field experience" plus a "skill-building activity tied to a specific curriculum standard" — the skill could state directly that the skill-building activity should generally use explicit teaching (modelling, guided practice) before students apply it independently in the investigation, consistent with AERO's and Kirschner et al.'s findings on novice learners.
2. **Add a line in Step 1 or the unit header naming which Gold Standard elements the unit is committing to.** The skill already asks about audience, product form, duration, and student agency (Step 1) but doesn't explicitly check for "Critique & Revision" or "Reflection" as designed-in elements — Phase 4 ("Going Further") includes peer critique as an example activity but it's optional phrasing ("Peer critique / feedback protocol"), not a required element. Given Gold Standard PBL treats critique & revision as one of seven essential (not optional) elements, the skill could make it a required inclusion rather than a bullet example.
3. **Add an implementation-difficulty caveat drawing on Condliffe et al.'s findings, especially for maths-primary units.** Since the MDRC review found weaker evidence and greater implementation difficulty for maths PBL specifically, the skill could flag — when the primary subject is Mathematics — that milestones and teacher check-ins (already present in Phase 2) may need to be more frequent than for humanities/science-anchored units, where the evidence base is stronger.
4. **Soften "Taking Action" (Phase 6) framing to avoid token action, using the skill's own instinct but naming why.** The skill already says "Not token action" — this is good, but could be strengthened with a line noting that Gold Standard PBL treats authenticity and public product as necessary conditions for PBL's effectiveness claims to apply; a nominal audience (e.g. "share with the class") does not carry the same evidence backing as a genuine external audience, so Step 1's audience question deserves more weight in the final unit than a single line item.

## Caveats / contested evidence
This is genuinely contested territory and the skill's framing ("The difference between good PBL and busy-work PBL...") already shows awareness of this, but doesn't cite why. On the supportive side: PBLWorks' Gold Standard model is the field-standard design specification (and this skill's structure closely tracks it), and Hattie's synthesis shows a moderate-to-strong effect for problem-based learning generally (d = 0.53, "potential to accelerate"). On the cautionary side: Condliffe et al.'s (2017) MDRC review — one of the most careful, non-advocacy literature reviews on K-12 PBL — found the evidence "promising but not proven," with weaker results in maths/literacy and real implementation burden on teachers; and the broader Kirschner, Sweller & Clark (2006) / Alfieri et al. (2011) evidence on minimally guided instruction applies to PBL's investigation phases just as it does to IBL's, since PBL is explicitly a form of inquiry with a product attached. AERO's Australian position — foundational knowledge first, inquiry/application after — implies PBL units work best when the "Finding Out" phase is more teacher-structured than a pure project-based purist model would prescribe, especially for topics genuinely new to students. None of this means PBL doesn't work; it means the skill should treat "well-scaffolded PBL with strong milestones" as the evidence-backed version, and should be explicit that a loosely-facilitated, minimally-scaffolded project is the version the evidence is least kind to.
