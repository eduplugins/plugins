# IBL Unit Creator — Evidence Base

**Skill file:** `plugins/skills/inquiry/ibl-unit/SKILL.md`
**Category:** Inquiry

## What the skill does
Builds a complete inquiry-based learning unit around a central inquiry question and a five-phase inquiry cycle (Tuning In, Finding Out, Sorting Out, Going Further, Drawing Conclusions), letting the teacher choose a structured, guided, or open inquiry model — with the teacher owning the question in structured/guided modes and students generating their own questions in open mode — and integrating curriculum links and differentiation throughout.

## Evidence base

### Visible Learning MetaX — Inquiry-based teaching
- **Publisher/author:** Corwin / John Hattie (Hattie holds a professorial position at the University of Melbourne)
- **Year:** Ongoing meta-analytic database; effect size figures current as of 2026 access
- **URL:** https://www.visiblelearningmetax.com/influences/view/inquiry-based_teaching
- **Australian source:** No (international database), but its lead researcher is Melbourne-based and it is widely cited in Australian school evidence policy
- **What it says:** Across 12 meta-analyses (431 studies, ~19,200 students), inquiry-based teaching — defined as a student-centred approach where the teacher guides students through questions posed, methods designed, and data interpreted by students — has a weighted mean effect size of 0.51, above Hattie's 0.40 "hinge point" for a year's expected growth, indicating a genuine positive but moderate effect.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Provides quantitative support for the "guided" model this skill defaults teachers toward ("Most classes work best with guided inquiry to start," Step 1) — the definition backing this effect size (teacher-guided questions/methods, student-interpreted data) matches the skill's own "Guided" inquiry description almost exactly, not the fully open model.

### Why Minimal Guidance During Instruction Does Not Work: An Analysis of the Failure of Constructivist, Discovery, Problem-Based, Experiential, and Inquiry-Based Teaching
- **Publisher/author:** Paul A. Kirschner, John Sweller, Richard E. Clark; *Educational Psychologist*, 41(2), 75–86
- **Year:** 2006
- **URL:** https://itgs.ict.usc.edu/papers/Constructivism_KirschnerEtAl_EP_06.pdf
- **Australian source:** No
- **What it says:** Minimally guided instructional approaches (including inquiry-based teaching in its unguided form) conflict with how working memory functions for novice learners, who lack the prior knowledge to self-generate effective problem-solving strategies; guided instruction produces better immediate recall and better long-term transfer, though the advantage recedes as learners gain expertise.
- **Relevance to this skill:** A direct, load-bearing caution against the "Open" inquiry model this skill offers as an option in Step 1 for students without strong prior knowledge or investigation skills — the paper's core claim is precisely that fully student-directed question generation and method design (what this skill calls "Open" inquiry) is the condition under which minimally guided instruction fails novices.

### Does discovery-based instruction enhance learning? (meta-analysis)
- **Publisher/author:** Louis Alfieri, Patricia J. Brooks, Naomi J. Aldrich, Harriet R. Tenenbaum; *Journal of Educational Psychology*, 103(1), 1–18
- **Year:** 2011
- **URL:** https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ933606
- **Australian source:** No
- **What it says:** Across 164 studies, unassisted discovery learning underperformed explicit instruction (d = −0.38), but "enhanced" discovery — supported with feedback, worked examples, scaffolding, and elicited explanations — outperformed other instruction types (d = 0.30), with guided discovery specifically showing the strongest benefit (d = 0.50) among enhancement types.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Offers the clearest evidence-based justification for the skill's three-tier inquiry model (Structured/Guided/Open) rather than a single "inquiry" approach — it empirically distinguishes exactly the difference the skill asks teachers to choose between in Step 1, and supports "Guided" as the strongest-performing form when done with scaffolding (worked examples, elicited explanation) rather than left open.

### Explicit instruction (explainer)
- **Publisher/author:** AERO (Australian Education Research Organisation)
- **Year:** September 2023
- **URL:** https://www.edresearch.edu.au/summaries-explainers/explainers/explicit-instruction
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Drawing on cognitive load theory and Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, AERO states guided instruction produces better immediate recall and longer-term transfer than unguided approaches, particularly for novices, though the guidance should reduce as expertise develops; AERO's broader position (per its other materials) is that inquiry is valuable *after* explicit instruction has built foundational knowledge, for extending and applying that knowledge to authentic tasks.
- **Relevance to this skill:** This is the specific Australian government-research-body position an Australian teacher using this skill would be weighed against — it does not reject inquiry, but positions it as most effective once foundational knowledge is explicitly taught, which has direct implications for how "Phase 2: Finding Out" should be scaffolded, especially in Guided and Open modes.

## How this evidence should sharpen the skill
1. **Make Step 1's inquiry-model recommendation evidence-based, not just a default.** The current line — "Most classes work best with guided inquiry to start" — is a bare assertion. It could cite, in substance if not by name, that guided discovery shows the strongest effect among discovery variants (Alfieri et al., d = 0.50) and that open/unassisted inquiry carries real risk for students without strong prior knowledge (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark), giving teachers a reason rather than a rule of thumb.
2. **Add an explicit knowledge-building step before "Open" inquiry's question generation.** Currently, for Open inquiry, Phase 1 (Tuning In) moves straight from provocation to "students record their own inquiry questions." Given AERO's and Kirschner et al.'s position that novices need foundational knowledge before self-directed investigation is productive, the skill could recommend a brief explicit knowledge-building activity before open question generation, especially where the topic is new to students.
3. **Flag in Phase 2 ("Finding Out") that "teacher as co-inquirer" (Open inquiry) still requires scaffolding.** The skill currently describes the Open inquiry teacher role only as "co-inquirer and resource connector," with no scaffolding language. Alfieri et al.'s finding that *enhanced* discovery (with worked examples and elicited explanation) outperforms unassisted discovery suggests this description should explicitly retain some scaffolding role even in Open mode — full withdrawal of guidance is not what the strongest-performing "open" studies looked like.
4. **Add a short caveat line near Step 1's inquiry-model question.** Given how contested this territory is, a one-line note such as "this is genuinely debated territory — guided approaches generally show stronger and more consistent evidence than fully open inquiry for students without strong prior knowledge of the topic" would let teachers make an informed choice rather than treating Structured/Guided/Open as equally-evidenced options.

## Caveats / contested evidence
This is squarely contested territory and the skill should treat it as such rather than presenting inquiry-based learning as uniformly effective. On the supportive side: Hattie's synthesis (via Visible Learning MetaX) finds a moderate positive effect for inquiry-based teaching (d = 0.51) when it is *teacher-guided*, and ACARA's Critical and Creative Thinking general capability explicitly builds "inquiring" into the Australian Curriculum as a general capability to be developed. On the cautionary side: Kirschner, Sweller & Clark (2006) is one of the most-cited papers in educational psychology arguing minimally guided approaches fail novice learners, and Alfieri et al.'s (2011) meta-analysis found *unassisted* discovery underperforms explicit instruction while only *enhanced/guided* discovery outperforms it. A 2026 critique published on the AARE's EduResearch Matters blog (https://blog.aare.edu.au/aero-says-educators-can-trust-its-evidence-can-they-really/) argues AERO itself over-simplifies this into a false binary and that "contextual factors must inform pedagogical choices" — a useful reminder that even the cautionary evidence has its own critics. Net effect for this skill: the weight of evidence favours guided over open inquiry for most classes (which the skill's own Step 1 language already leans toward), and the skill would be strengthened by making that lean explicit and evidence-based rather than an unexplained default.
