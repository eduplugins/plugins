# Student Task Sheet Creator — Evidence Base

**Skill file:** `plugins/skills/planning/student-task-sheet/SKILL.md`
**Category:** Planning

## What the skill does
Translates a teacher's lesson plan or assessment task into a student-facing handout: step-by-step instructions in plain, active-voice language, success criteria rewritten in student "I can..." language, optional sentence starters/scaffolds, a vocabulary glossary, and a specific (not generic) extension task for students who finish early.

## Evidence base

### AERO Practice guide — Scaffold practice
- **Publisher/author:** Australian Education Research Organisation (AERO)
- **Year:** 2024
- **URL:** https://www.edresearch.edu.au/sites/default/files/2024-11/AERO-practice-guide-scaffold-practice-aa.pdf
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Sets out scaffolding techniques including worked examples (modelling each step aloud before moving to the next, keeping steps clear to manage cognitive load), process worksheets that guide students through a method, and annotated work samples that show students what success looks like — with guidance that scaffolds should be gradually reduced as students improve (the "fading" principle).
- **Relevance to this skill:** Directly supports the task sheet's "Sentence starters / scaffolds" section and the instruction to write success criteria in a way that makes "what good looks like" concrete for students — AERO's annotated-work-sample guidance is essentially what the "What good looks like" section is trying to achieve in text form.

### Sharing learning intentions and success criteria
- **Publisher/author:** NSW Department of Education (explicit teaching technique guide)
- **Year:** 2025
- **URL:** https://education.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/main-education/documents/teaching-and-learning/curriculum/explicit-teaching/explicit-teaching-technique-guide-lisc-sharing.pdf
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Learning intentions and success criteria are not just delivered once at the start of a lesson — they are explained, checked for understanding, and referred back to throughout the lesson to guide and formatively assess learning.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Backs the task sheet's dedicated "What we are learning" and "What good looks like (Success criteria)" sections, and its instruction that success criteria should be rewritten in student language rather than copied verbatim from curriculum-facing documents — the point is for students to actually use them during the task, not just read them once.

### Australian Professional Standards for Teachers — Standard 3: Plan for and implement effective teaching and learning
- **Publisher/author:** Australian Institute for Teaching and School Leadership (AITSL)
- **Year:** Standards current; accessed 2026
- **URL:** https://www.aitsl.edu.au/standards
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Standard 3.1 requires teachers to set explicit, challenging and achievable learning goals for all students; Standard 3.3 requires selecting and using teaching strategies that develop knowledge, skills and thinking; the broader standard requires teaching resources that are appropriately structured for the learner.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Provides the professional-standards basis for the skill's core design principle that student-facing materials must be shaped for the audience — "from curriculum language to student language, from teacher-facing structure to student-navigable steps" is a direct operationalisation of what Standard 3 requires of teacher-produced learning resources.

### Principles of Instruction: Research-Based Strategies That All Teachers Should Know
- **Publisher/author:** Barak Rosenshine, American Educator (American Federation of Teachers)
- **Year:** 2012 (originally published by the International Academy of Education, 2010)
- **URL:** https://www.aft.org/ae/spring2012/rosenshine
- **Australian source:** No (international, but very widely cited in Australian teacher PD, including by AERO)
- **What it says:** Principle 4 recommends providing models and worked examples for students, especially novices, before independent practice; the principles also stress giving clear, explicit instructions and checking understanding before students work alone — closely related to Sweller's worked-example effect from cognitive load theory.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Supports the task sheet's numbered, single-instruction-per-step structure ("One instruction per step") and its emphasis on scaffolds/sentence starters for tasks with a language demand — both are ways of keeping a student's independent working memory load manageable while they work without direct teacher support.

## How this evidence should sharpen the skill
1. **Ground the "success criteria in student language" instruction explicitly.** The skill states success criteria should "Match the lesson's success criteria — rewritten for a student reader" but doesn't explain why the rewrite matters beyond readability. Given the NSW DoE LISC guide's point that success criteria need to be *referred back to* during the task, consider adding a line encouraging teachers to phrase success criteria so students can literally check themselves against them mid-task (e.g. as a checklist), not just read them once at the top.
2. **Add a fading/tiering note to the Scaffolds section.** AERO's scaffold-practice guide stresses that scaffolds should reduce as proficiency increases. The current Step 3 planning question ("does this task need sentence starters or scaffolds for students who need support?") treats scaffolding as binary (include/omit). Consider a lightweight addition — e.g. "If this task follows earlier ones in a unit, ask whether scaffolds should be lighter than last time" — so the skill doesn't default to the same level of support every time it's invoked.
3. **Strengthen the worked-example option for procedural/analytical tasks.** Rosenshine's and AERO's guidance both treat worked examples as a distinct, often more effective scaffold than sentence starters for procedural or analytical tasks (e.g. maths problems, text analysis). The current "Sentence starters / scaffolds" section only offers sentence-starter examples; consider explicitly branching this section — sentence starters for writing/language tasks, a worked first step for procedural/analytical tasks — so the skill doesn't default to a language scaffold when a modelled step would serve better.
4. **Make the "Finished early?" extension section's rationale explicit.** The skill already insists this be "Something that extends the thinking of the task itself" rather than generic filler — this is good practice but currently unsupported by a cited rationale in the file. A short note (even just internal, in the Principles-equivalent guidance) referencing that effective extension work should deepen the same learning goal rather than introduce unrelated content would tie this back to AITSL Standard 3.1's requirement for challenging, goal-aligned tasks for all students.

## Caveats / contested evidence
None identified as directly contested. One limitation worth noting: the evidence base here (scaffolding, worked examples, success criteria clarity) is drawn mostly from lesson- and unit-level teaching research; there is little research specifically on the *document format* of a standalone student task sheet as a genre. The skill's structural choices (numbered steps, a vocabulary table, a "how this will be assessed" statement) are a reasonable translation of the underlying pedagogical evidence into a document format, but that translation itself is the skill's own design work rather than something separately evidenced in the literature.
