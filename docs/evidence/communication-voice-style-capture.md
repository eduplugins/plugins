# Voice & Style Capture — Evidence Base

**Skill file:** `plugins/skills/communication/voice-style-capture/SKILL.md`
**Category:** Communication

## What the skill does
Analyses samples of a teacher's own writing (tone, sentence patterns, vocabulary, structural habits) to build a reusable voice profile, so that AI-drafted report comments and parent communications sound like the teacher wrote them personally rather than reading as generic or AI-generated.

## Evidence base

### Parental involvement to parental engagement: a continuum
- **Publisher/author:** Janet Goodall & Caroline Montgomery
- **Year:** 2014, *Educational Review*, Vol 66, No 4
- **URL:** https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1039779
- **Australian source:** No — international, UK
- **What it says:** Distinguishes "parental involvement" (participation in school activities and generic school-to-home messaging) from "parental engagement" (direct, relationship-based connection to a child's learning), finding the greatest benefit sits at the engagement end of the continuum, and that movement along the continuum depends on both parent and school actions.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Provides the closest research grounding for the skill's central premise — that communication which feels personal and relationship-specific (a genuine teacher voice) sits further along the engagement continuum than generic, uniform school messaging, and is therefore more likely to build the kind of partnership the continuum associates with better outcomes.

### Engaging parents/carers (Illustration of Practice, Standard 7.3)
- **Publisher/author:** AITSL — Australian Professional Standards for Teachers, Focus Area 7.3
- **Year:** Undated (current AITSL resource)
- **URL:** https://www.aitsl.edu.au/tools-resources/resource/engaging-parents-carers-illustration-of-practice
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Describes effective parent/carer engagement as combining formal, accurate reporting with an ongoing, personally-invested relationship the teacher builds deliberately — not a one-off or templated interaction.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Supports the skill's framing that voice capture exists "so that a report comment or letter home reflects [the teacher's] relationship with their students and families — not a template," aligning with AITSL's own illustration of what proficient engagement looks like in practice.

### Working with Parents to Support Children's Learning (Guidance Report)
- **Publisher/author:** Education Endowment Foundation (EEF), UK
- **Year:** 2018
- **URL:** https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/supporting-parents
- **Australian source:** No — international, UK
- **What it says:** Recommends schools "tailor school communications" — finding personalised communication, rather than generic broadcast messaging, is more likely to encourage parental engagement.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Backs the underlying assumption that personalisation (of which a matched teacher voice is one form) makes communication more effective, though the report is about tailoring to the *family's* needs rather than preserving the *teacher's* individual writing style specifically.

## How this evidence should sharpen the skill
1. **State the "why" up front using the engagement continuum.** The skill's opening framing ("The goal is not mimicry for its own sake...") could explicitly reference the involvement/engagement distinction — voice capture exists to keep communications on the "engagement" end (personal, relational) rather than sliding toward generic templated "involvement"-style messaging, which would sharpen the pedagogical justification for teachers unfamiliar with why tone-matching matters beyond aesthetics.
2. **Add a caveat that voice-matching is not itself a validated intervention.** None of the evidence found directly studies AI-assisted voice capture or tone-matching as a technique — the skill should be explicit (perhaps in a short note near Step 2) that the practice is a reasonable extrapolation from broader personalisation research, not a directly tested method, so teachers don't over-trust it as "evidence-based" in a narrower sense than it is.
3. **Tie the "Avoid: generic openers" analysis (Step 2) to the EEF framing.** Step 2 already asks the model to note phrases the teacher never uses — this could be explicitly framed as identifying and avoiding the generic, non-personalised patterns that EEF/Goodall & Montgomery associate with lower-engagement communication (e.g. the classic "Name is a pleasure to have in class" opener named as an example in Step 3), making the "why avoid generic openers" rationale concrete rather than purely stylistic.
4. **Cross-reference AITSL 7.3 in the saved voice-profile file template.** The generated `[teacher-name]-voice-profile` SKILL.md (Step 5) could include a short line noting the profile exists to support "reporting clearly, accurately and respectfully" (AITSL 5.5/7.3 language) in the teacher's own voice — giving the generated artefact a stated professional rationale, not just a stylistic one.

## Caveats / contested evidence
There is no direct empirical research on AI-assisted "voice capture" or tone-matching for teacher communications — the evidence base here is inferential, built from general findings that personalised, relationship-based communication outperforms generic messaging (Goodall & Montgomery; EEF), not from studies of this specific technique. The skill's claim that matching a teacher's voice makes communication "reflect their relationship with their students and families" is a reasonable but untested extension of that broader research, and should be treated as a design rationale rather than a directly evidenced outcome.
