# Feedback Comment Bank — Evidence Base

**Skill file:** `plugins/skills/assessment/feedback-comment-bank/SKILL.md`
**Category:** Assessment

## What the skill does
Generates a bank of reusable, criterion-specific feedback phrases organised by rubric criterion and grade band, grounded in the rubric or curriculum achievement standards (never invented from scratch), varied in wording so teachers aren't copy-pasting identical comments across a class set, and adaptable to a teacher's own voice profile.

## Evidence base

### Hattie, J. & Timperley, H. (2007) — The Power of Feedback
- **Publisher/author:** John Hattie and Helen Timperley; published in *Review of Educational Research*, Vol. 77(1), pp. 81–112 (American Educational Research Association)
- **Year:** 2007
- **URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3102/003465430298487
- **Australian source:** No — international, but John Hattie is based at the University of Melbourne, giving this a strong Australian institutional connection despite the paper itself being published internationally
- **What it says:** Argues feedback is one of the most powerful influences on learning and achievement, but that this effect "can be either positive or negative" — the specific type of feedback and how it is delivered determines whether it helps or hinders. Feedback works best when it reduces the gap between current performance and a learning goal.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Directly supports the skill's insistence that phrases be "specific to the criterion" and "actionable" rather than generic ("well done"), since Hattie & Timperley's model treats feedback as only effective when it is precise enough to close a specific gap — vague praise doesn't meet that bar.

### Education Endowment Foundation (2021) — Teacher Feedback to Improve Pupil Learning
- **Publisher/author:** Joe Collin and Alex Quigley, Education Endowment Foundation (EEF)
- **Year:** 2021
- **URL:** https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED615988.pdf
- **Australian source:** No — international (UK), but a widely-used evidence-based guidance report in Australian schools too
- **What it says:** Sets out six recommendations built on three core principles: lay the foundations for effective feedback through high-quality initial teaching; deliver appropriately-timed feedback focused on moving learning forward; and plan for how pupils will actually receive and use the feedback given. It explicitly moves the debate away from written-vs-verbal delivery and toward the *principles* of good feedback.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Supports the skill's emphasis on feedback being "actionable (the student knows what to do differently)" — the EEF's second and third principles are precisely about feedback that moves learning forward and is designed to be acted on, not just delivered.

### What Works Best 2025 practical guide — Effective feedback
- **Publisher/author:** NSW Department of Education, Centre for Education Statistics and Evaluation (CESE)
- **Year:** 2025 (December)
- **URL:** https://education.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/main-education/about-us/educational-data/cese/What_Works_Best_2025_Effective_feedback_practical_guide.pdf
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Defines effective feedback as "timely, actionable and specific information provided to students that aims to communicate the gap between current and desired performance... as well as how to close this gap," citing Hattie & Timperley (2007) and Hattie (2009) among others, and notes it "should align with the learning intention and success criteria of a task."
- **Relevance to this skill:** This is an almost word-for-word match to the skill's own definition of "good feedback phrases" (specific, actionable). It gives the skill's design brief a current, NSW-government-endorsed source, and confirms the practice of grounding feedback in stated criteria (which the skill implements by anchoring every phrase to a rubric criterion).

### Australian Professional Standards for Teachers — Standard 5.2
- **Publisher/author:** Australian Institute for Teaching and School Leadership (AITSL)
- **Year:** Current standards (live reference document)
- **URL:** https://www.aitsl.edu.au/standards
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Standard 5.2 (Graduate career stage) requires teachers to "Demonstrate an understanding of the purpose of providing timely and appropriate feedback to students about their learning."
- **Relevance to this skill:** Establishes that producing timely, well-targeted feedback is a formal professional requirement for Australian teachers, not just good practice — supporting the skill's practical goal of making it faster to generate feedback that still meets this professional expectation across a full class set.

## How this evidence should sharpen the skill
1. **Add a rationale line for the "vary sentence structure" instruction.** The skill asks for varied phrasing "so teachers aren't copying the same phrase across 30 scripts" but doesn't explain why that matters pedagogically (it isn't just about avoiding tedium — copy-pasted, generic phrases risk becoming exactly the low-specificity comments Hattie & Timperley and EEF warn reduce feedback's power). Consider making that link explicit in the opening framing.
2. **Strengthen the "honest about both strength and growth" instruction with an actionability check.** The current Band 4/5 descriptors ("names what's present AND what's missing," "focuses on what the student can do to move forward") already reflect EEF's "move learning forward" principle well. Consider adding an explicit quality-check step (similar to Rubric Builder's "Quality check before delivering") asking: does each phrase tell the student a *specific next action*, not just a diagnosis?
3. **Flag the risk of feedback overload.** EEF's guidance stresses planning "for how pupils will receive and use feedback" — a comment bank that makes it easy to generate many detailed phrases per student could tempt teachers into information-dense reports that students can't act on. Consider adding a short caution in Step 3 or the teacher-facing next-steps note recommending teachers select 1–2 priority phrases per student rather than compiling every applicable comment.
4. **Cite the evidence base directly in the skill's opening paragraph**, similar to how the current text asserts (without citation) that "good feedback phrases are: specific... actionable... varied... honest." A short footnote-style reference to Hattie & Timperley (2007) and the EEF (2021) guidance report would let teachers know these aren't just house-style preferences.

## Caveats / contested evidence
Hattie & Timperley's own model (feed up / feed back / feed forward, across task, process, self-regulation, and self levels) cautions that feedback aimed at the *self* level (generic praise of the person, e.g. "well done," "great effort") is typically the least effective form and can even be counterproductive — the skill's "General opening phrases" list is careful to avoid this (its openers name what the student *did*, e.g. "You have demonstrated..."), which is a good alignment worth preserving as the skill evolves. Separately, the EEF report notes feedback effectiveness varies considerably by context and subject, so the guidance should be read as general principles rather than a guarantee that any given phrase bank will improve outcomes — a caveat the skill does not currently state.
