# Parent Curriculum Explainer — Evidence Base

**Skill file:** `plugins/skills/communication/parent-curriculum-explainer/SKILL.md`
**Category:** Communication

## What the skill does
Translates official curriculum content descriptions and achievement standards into plain-English explanations for families, covering what students are learning, why it matters, what it looks like in class, and how families can help at home — always fetched live from the real curriculum (never quoted verbatim, never paraphrased from memory) and formatted for newsletters, curriculum-night handouts, or letters home.

## Evidence base

### Parent/carer information (V9 Australian Curriculum)
- **Publisher/author:** ACARA (Australian Curriculum, Assessment and Reporting Authority)
- **Year:** Current (V9 Australian Curriculum site)
- **URL:** https://www.australiancurriculum.edu.au/help/parent-information
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** States that "parents and carers can support their child's education by familiarising themselves with the Australian Curriculum to gain a general understanding of what their child is learning at each stage of their schooling," and points parents to achievement standards and work samples as the primary way to understand expectations at each year level.
- **Relevance to this skill:** This is ACARA's own stated purpose for family-facing curriculum communication — it validates the skill's core premise that translating achievement standards and content descriptions for families (rather than leaving them in technical form) is the officially endorsed way to help parents support learning.

### Australian Professional Standards for Teachers — Focus Area 5.5 (Report on student achievement)
- **Publisher/author:** AITSL
- **Year:** Current standard
- **URL:** https://www.aitsl.edu.au/standards
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** At Proficient level, teachers must "report clearly, accurately and respectfully to students and parents/carers about student achievement," which requires translating assessment and curriculum information into a form the audience can understand and act on.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Backs the skill's insistence on avoiding jargon, acronyms, and content-description codes (Step 4) — the professional standard itself requires clarity and respect for the audience, not technical fidelity to curriculum-document language.

### Do parents know they matter? Engaging all parents in learning
- **Publisher/author:** Alma Harris & Janet Goodall, University of Warwick / Department for Children, Schools and Families (UK)
- **Year:** 2007 (DCSF research report); related 2008 journal article in *Educational Research*, Vol 50, No 3
- **URL:** https://dera.ioe.ac.uk/6639/1/DCSF-RW004.pdf
- **Australian source:** No — international, UK
- **What it says:** Based on case-study data from 20 schools, the research found that engaging parents directly *in their child's learning* (not just in school activities) makes the greatest difference to achievement, and that many "hard to reach" parents are in fact poorly reached by schools rather than disengaged by choice.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Supports the skill's "how you can help at home" section as the single most evidence-backed component of a curriculum explainer — the research specifically identifies engagement with learning-at-home content, not general school involvement, as the outcome-linked factor.

### Working with Parents to Support Children's Learning (Guidance Report)
- **Publisher/author:** Education Endowment Foundation (EEF), UK
- **Year:** 2018
- **URL:** https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/supporting-parents
- **Australian source:** No — international, UK
- **What it says:** Recommends that schools "tailor school communications to encourage parental engagement" and give parents specific, actionable ways to support learning at home rather than generic encouragement.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Directly supports the skill's "How can you help at home?" section design, which the SKILL.md requires to be "specific, doable" rather than vague — this matches EEF's finding that specific, low-burden suggestions are more likely to be acted on than general encouragement.

## How this evidence should sharpen the skill
1. **Name the achievement-standard translation duty explicitly.** Step 4's checklist bans phrases like "achievement standards" and "content descriptions" appearing in output, but the skill could add a one-line rationale (citing AITSL 5.5 in spirit) that this is a *professional reporting obligation to communicate clearly*, not merely a style preference — this would help a teacher understand why the acronym-ban is non-negotiable rather than optional polish.
2. **Strengthen the "how can you help at home" prompt using Harris & Goodall.** Step 3's newsletter/handout templates ask for "1–2 specific, doable suggestions" — the skill could tighten this by explicitly steering toward suggestions that connect to the child's *current, specific* learning task (as the research shows learning-focused engagement outperforms general school involvement) rather than generic "read together" advice.
3. **Add a CALD-specific home-support prompt.** Step 1 already asks about CALD/literacy context, but Step 3's "how you can help at home" suggestions are English-language-literacy dependent (e.g. "talk about it in the news"). Given the family-engagement evidence is strongest when suggestions are low-burden and accessible, the skill could add guidance to offer suggestions that don't require English fluency (e.g. conversation prompts in home language, non-text activities).
4. **Reference the official ACARA parent-information page as the "ground truth" check.** Step 2 already mandates fetching real curriculum content via MCP/BASE_URL; it could additionally point teachers unsure of tone toward ACARA's own parent-information page as a benchmark for what ACARA itself considers appropriately pitched for families.

## Caveats / contested evidence
The Harris & Goodall and EEF evidence is UK-based and about parental engagement broadly, not specifically about curriculum-explainer documents as a communication format — there is no controlled study evaluating "curriculum night handouts" or "newsletter blurbs" as an intervention in their own right, so the skill's specific format choices are a reasonable extrapolation from broader family-engagement evidence rather than a directly tested design. The ACARA parent-information source establishes intent and endorsement but is not itself impact-evaluated research.
