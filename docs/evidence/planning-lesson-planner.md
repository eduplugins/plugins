# Lesson Planner — Evidence Base

**Skill file:** `plugins/skills/planning/lesson-planner/SKILL.md`
**Category:** Planning

## What the skill does
Creates a single, curriculum-aligned lesson plan by fetching live curriculum content (never inventing standards), asking a small set of planning questions, and generating a structured plan with lesson goals, student-facing success criteria, an Introduction → Body → Closure teaching sequence built around explicit "I do / We do / You do" modelling, differentiation, resources, and assessment — optionally reshaped by a chosen pedagogy framework (UbD, Visible Learning, HITS, Explicit Teaching, UDL, IBL).

## Evidence base

### What Works Best (evidence guide for excellent schools)
- **Publisher/author:** Centre for Education Statistics and Evaluation (CESE), NSW Department of Education
- **Year:** Framework originated 2014, updated 2020, current "2025" evidence guide edition (page last updated 2026)
- **URL:** http://education.nsw.gov.au/about-us/education-data-and-research/what-works-best/what-works-best-2025-evidence-guide-for-excellent-schools
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Identifies eight evidence-based teaching practices with the strongest evidence of improving student learning outcomes: high expectations, explicit teaching, effective feedback, use of data to inform practice, assessment, classroom management, wellbeing, and collaboration.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Backs the skill's emphasis on explicit lesson goals/success criteria, a planned teaching sequence with a formative check ("Assessment / Evidence of Learning"), and differentiation — these map directly onto the "explicit teaching," "assessment," and "high expectations" themes. Note: the original "2020 update" URL now redirects to this 2025 evidence guide, which is the current live version of the same CESE framework — see caveat below.

### Explicit teaching drives student motivation, engagement, and achievement in NSW public schools (What Works Best research update)
- **Publisher/author:** Centre for Education Statistics and Evaluation (CESE), NSW Department of Education
- **Year:** Republished 2026 (originally part of the 2020 What Works Best update lineage)
- **URL:** https://education.nsw.gov.au/about-us/education-data-and-research/cese/publications/research-reports/what-works-best-2020-update/explicit-teaching-wwb-research-update
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Explicit teaching and feedback are 2 of the 8 What Works Best practices; survey data from NSW public high schools shows explicit teaching improves learning outcomes in the short and long term and fosters student motivation and engagement.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Directly supports the "Explicit Teaching" pedagogy framework option in the skill, which structures the teaching sequence strictly as I Do → We Do → You Do with a modelling script or worked example.

### Australian Professional Standards for Teachers — Standard 3: Plan for and implement effective teaching and learning
- **Publisher/author:** Australian Institute for Teaching and School Leadership (AITSL)
- **Year:** Standards current; AITSL site accessed 2026
- **URL:** https://www.aitsl.edu.au/standards
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Standard 3 requires teachers to set explicit, challenging and achievable learning goals (3.1); plan, structure and sequence well-structured learning and teaching programs (3.2); and select and use relevant teaching strategies to develop knowledge, skills, and critical/creative thinking (3.3).
- **Relevance to this skill:** This is the direct professional-standards basis for the lesson plan template's "Lesson goals," "Teaching Sequence," and differentiation sections — the skill's structure is essentially an operationalisation of Standard 3's focus areas into a fillable template.

### Principles of Instruction: Research-Based Strategies That All Teachers Should Know
- **Publisher/author:** Barak Rosenshine, American Educator (American Federation of Teachers)
- **Year:** 2012 (originally published by the International Academy of Education in 2010)
- **URL:** https://www.aft.org/ae/spring2012/rosenshine
- **Australian source:** No (international, but very widely cited in Australian teacher PD, including by AERO and CESE)
- **What it says:** Synthesises research from cognitive science, studies of master teachers, and cognitive-support research into ten principles, including reviewing prior learning, presenting new material in small steps with practice, providing models/worked examples, guiding student practice, and checking for understanding before independent practice.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Underpins the Body phase structure (explicit teaching/modelling → guided practice → independent/collaborative activity) that the skill uses as its default teaching sequence regardless of which pedagogy framework is selected.

## How this evidence should sharpen the skill
1. **Name the evidence base in the "Explicit Teaching" framework bullet.** The current line ("Structure the sequence strictly as I Do → We Do → You Do. Include a modelling script or worked example in the Body phase") states the practice but not why it's non-negotiable. A short parenthetical — "(after Rosenshine's Principles of Instruction and AERO's explicit instruction guidance)" — would make the design choice traceable for a sceptical school leader reviewing AI-generated plans.
2. **The default "no framework" teaching sequence already mirrors I Do/We Do/You Do implicitly** (see the Body phase bullets: "Explicit teaching / modelling," "Guided practice," "Independent … activity") but doesn't say so. Given What Works Best and Rosenshine both treat this progression as close to a floor requirement rather than one option among six, consider making the Body phase's gradual-release structure the explicit default even when no framework is selected, rather than only naming it under "Explicit Teaching."
3. **Success criteria section could ask for the check-for-understanding placement explicitly.** Rosenshine's principle 5 ("check for student understanding") and What Works Best's "assessment" theme both stress that checks should be *distributed through* the lesson, not just at Closure. The template's Closure section already includes an "Exit task or check for understanding" — worth adding a matching prompt in the Body phase (e.g. "planned check-in during guided practice") so formative checks aren't only end-loaded.
4. **AITSL Standard 3.2 explicitly requires programs that "engage students,"** but the skill's planning questions (max 5, combinable) don't currently ask about student engagement/motivation strategies directly — differentiation and real-world connection are asked, but not engagement design. Given What Works Best names "student motivation and engagement" as a distinct explicit-teaching outcome, a light-touch addition (folded into the existing "activities or strategies" question) could close this gap without adding a new question.

## Caveats / contested evidence
The original "What Works Best: 2020 update" URL has since been superseded/redirected by NSW DoE to a "What Works Best 2025 evidence guide" — the eight practice themes are unchanged, but anyone citing "the 2020 update" by name should be aware the department now presents this as a living, updated resource rather than a fixed 2020 document. Separately, Rosenshine's principles are drawn largely from correlational studies of "master teachers" plus cognitive-load research, not from randomised trials of the whole ten-principle package as a single intervention — the underlying components (worked examples, spaced practice, checking for understanding) are individually well-evidenced, but the specific sequencing recommended by the skill (strict I Do → We Do → You Do) is a widely-adopted synthesis rather than something itself directly tested as a unit.
