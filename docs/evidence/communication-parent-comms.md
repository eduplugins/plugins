# Parent Comms Writer — Evidence Base

**Skill file:** `plugins/skills/communication/parent-comms/SKILL.md`
**Category:** Communication

## What the skill does
Drafts parent-facing written communications (newsletters, curriculum updates, excursion notes, learning updates, concern letters) that read as if the teacher wrote them personally. It emphasises plain English, warmth, specificity over generic positivity, and accessibility for families with diverse language and literacy backgrounds, with extra care taken around "learning concern" letters.

## Evidence base

### Spotlight: Strengthening parent engagement to improve student outcomes
- **Publisher/author:** AITSL (Australian Institute for Teaching and School Leadership)
- **Year:** 2024
- **URL:** https://www.aitsl.edu.au/research/spotlights/strengthening-parent-engagement-to-improve-student-outcomes
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Reviews decades of research showing that when parents engage with their child's learning, student achievement, wellbeing, behaviour and attendance all improve; stresses that "consistent and genuine communication with parents, particularly small moments of connection," builds mutually beneficial partnerships, and that schools should communicate regularly — not only when problems arise — using approaches that account for language and socioeconomic barriers.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Directly backs the skill's premise that routine, warm, specific communication (newsletters, learning updates) is not just courtesy but a lever for outcomes, and supports the instruction to write for families with diverse backgrounds and engagement levels.

### Engaging parents/carers (Illustration of Practice, Standard 7.3)
- **Publisher/author:** AITSL — Australian Professional Standards for Teachers, Focus Area 7.3 ("Engage with the parents/carers") and 5.5 ("Report on student achievement")
- **Year:** Undated (current AITSL resource)
- **URL:** https://www.aitsl.edu.au/tools-resources/resource/engaging-parents-carers-illustration-of-practice
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Illustrates a Proficient-level teacher reporting "clearly, accurately and respectfully" to parents/carers, using specific work samples rather than vague summaries, and treating parent engagement as an explicit, ongoing goal rather than a one-off task.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Grounds the skill's "specific — vague positivity is forgettable" tone principle and the requirement that concern letters describe specific, evidence-based behaviour — this is the exact professional standard Australian teachers are assessed against for written reporting.

### Working with Parents to Support Children's Learning (Guidance Report)
- **Publisher/author:** Education Endowment Foundation (EEF), UK
- **Year:** 2018
- **URL:** https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/guidance-reports/supporting-parents
- **Australian source:** No — international, UK
- **What it says:** One of four evidence-based recommendations is to "tailor school communications to encourage parental engagement" — the report finds communication is more effective when personalised, positively framed, and free of inaccessible language or professional jargon.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Directly supports Step 5's plain-English/no-jargon rule and the instruction to simplify for CALD families and varied home literacy levels — this is the closest widely-cited evidence base specifically on *how* school-to-family communication should be written, which Australian sources describe less prescriptively.

### Strengthening family and community engagement in student learning resource
- **Publisher/author:** NSW Department of Education
- **Year:** Undated (hosted resource; the companion "options resource" is dated 2016)
- **URL:** https://education.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/main-education/en/home/teaching-and-learning/curriculum/multicultural-education/culture-and-diversity/engaging-communities/Strengthening_family_and_community_engagement_in_student_learning_resource.pdf
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** A toolkit for schools that names concrete CALD-inclusive communication strategies, including newsletters in families' first languages, help for families to understand school reports, and use of interpreters and varied channels (SMS, email, blogs) to reach families with different needs.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Supports the skill's practice of asking about CALD families/home literacy levels up front and adapting sentence length, idiom use, and assumed cultural knowledge accordingly.

## How this evidence should sharpen the skill
1. **Cite the standard, not just the instinct.** Step 5's tone principles ("specific," "accessible") could explicitly reference AITSL Standard 7.3/5.5 language ("report clearly, accurately and respectfully") so the skill's guidance reads as aligned to the actual professional standard Australian teachers are held to, not just house style.
2. **Add a communication-frequency nudge.** The AITSL spotlight stresses that positive, routine contact (not only concern-driven contact) has the biggest engagement payoff. The skill currently only prompts for content type when the teacher already knows what they want to send — it could add a light suggestion (e.g. in Step 2) to consider a positive/appreciative update if the only communications logged this term have been logistical or concern-based.
3. **Strengthen the CALD guidance with concrete strategies.** Step 2/5 currently say "simpler sentences, no idioms" — the NSW resource above suggests concretely offering a translated-summary line or naming an interpreter/contact option for families who may need it, which the skill could add as an optional field when CALD families are flagged.
4. **Ground the concern-letter caution in evidence.** The "handle with particular care" language in the Learning Concern Letter section could reference the EEF finding that personalised, positively-framed communication is more effective than generic language — reinforcing why the skill insists on behaviour-specific, non-labelling phrasing.

## Caveats / contested evidence
The EEF guidance report is UK-based, aimed largely at improving academic outcomes for disadvantaged families specifically — its recommendations on tailoring communications are well-evidenced for that population but the skill applies them universally to all families, which is a reasonable but broader extrapolation than the report itself makes. The AITSL and NSW sources describe *standards and toolkits*, not controlled studies, so they establish professional expectation rather than causal proof that a given letter template improves outcomes — appropriate for a professional-practice skill, but worth noting the evidence tier is different from the EEF report.
