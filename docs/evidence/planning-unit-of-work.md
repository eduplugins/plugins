# Unit of Work Creator — Evidence Base

**Skill file:** `plugins/skills/planning/unit-of-work/SKILL.md`
**Category:** Planning

## What the skill does
Expands a teacher-confirmed unit outline into a complete, lesson-by-lesson unit of work — mapping curriculum standards across the lesson sequence before writing any lesson, building each lesson with progressive goals (early lessons establish knowledge/skills, later lessons apply and synthesise), and refusing to generate full lesson detail until the outline shape itself has been validated by the teacher.

## Evidence base

### Sequencing learning
- **Publisher/author:** Australian Curriculum, Assessment and Reporting Authority (ACARA), V9 Australian Curriculum
- **Year:** Current resource, V9 (accessed 2026)
- **URL:** https://www.australiancurriculum.edu.au/resources/curriculum-leadership/eight-aspects-of-curriculum-planning/sequencing-learning
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Defines sequencing learning as organising "what is taught, when and in what order, so that students' knowledge, understanding and skills build logically and become increasingly sophisticated over time," and recommends whole-school curriculum maps that show this progression as the basis for individual unit planning.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Directly supports the skill's "Progression" rule — that lesson goals must build across the unit, with early lessons establishing knowledge/skills and later lessons applying and synthesising — this is ACARA's own definition of what sequencing a unit of work well looks like.

### Australian Professional Standards for Teachers — Standards 2 and 3
- **Publisher/author:** Australian Institute for Teaching and School Leadership (AITSL)
- **Year:** Standards current; accessed 2026
- **URL:** https://www.aitsl.edu.au/standards
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Standard 2.2 requires teachers to "organise content into an effective learning and teaching sequence"; Standard 2.3 requires using curriculum, assessment and reporting knowledge to design learning sequences; Standard 3.2 requires teachers to "plan, structure and sequence learning programs" using knowledge of student learning, content and effective teaching strategies.
- **Relevance to this skill:** These two standards together are the professional basis for building a unit of work as a coherent, curriculum-mapped sequence rather than a set of independent lessons — the skill's requirement to map curriculum content across all lessons before writing any of them, and to keep differentiation "named explicitly — never optional" in every lesson, operationalises 2.2/2.3/3.2 directly.

### Backward Design (Understanding by Design, Wiggins & McTighe)
- **Publisher/author:** Western Michigan University Center for Teaching, Learning and Assessment, summarising Wiggins & McTighe (1998), *Understanding by Design*, ASCD
- **Year:** Original work 1998 (ASCD); summary page current
- **URL:** https://wmich.edu/x/teaching-learning/teaching-resources/backward-design
- **Australian source:** No (international, but UbD is one of the pedagogy frameworks the skill itself explicitly supports)
- **What it says:** Backward design plans in three stages — (1) identify desired results/enduring understandings, (2) determine acceptable evidence of learning, (3) plan learning activities — deliberately reversing the traditional order of choosing activities first and goals last.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Backs the skill's own "always validate the unit outline before expanding" rule, and its instruction to map curriculum standards across lessons *before* writing lesson detail — this mirrors backward design's insistence that results and assessment evidence be fixed before instructional activities are planned. The skill also explicitly reflects on UbD fit via its pedagogy-framework step.

## How this evidence should sharpen the skill
1. **Cite the sequencing rationale in the "Progression" instruction.** The skill currently states "Lesson goals should build across the unit — early lessons establish knowledge and skills; later lessons apply, extend, or synthesise" as a bare rule. A short grounding phrase — "(this reflects ACARA's guidance on sequencing learning so that knowledge and skill become increasingly sophisticated over time)" — would make this traceable to curriculum-authority guidance rather than reading as house style.
2. **Strengthen the "map curriculum content to lessons" step with an explicit backward-design framing even when UbD isn't the chosen framework.** Currently, backward-design logic (map before you build) is baked into the skill's structure but is only named as a framework choice under "Pedagogy framework." Since the skill already enforces this sequence by default (fetch → confirm outline → map standards → build lessons), consider a one-line acknowledgement that this default order is itself a backward-design pattern — this would help a teacher understand *why* the skill refuses to build lessons before the mapping step, especially if they're in a hurry.
3. **Tie "differentiation... never optional" to AITSL Standard 1 (know students) alongside Standard 3.** The skill correctly treats differentiation as mandatory per lesson, but currently cites no standard for this. AITSL Standard 1 (Know students and how they learn) is the natural companion to Standard 3 here — even a passing reference in the wrapper's "Differentiation Overview" instructions (e.g. "reflect how support and extension respond to what's known about these students") would connect the requirement to its actual professional basis rather than leaving it as an unexplained mandate.
4. **Make curriculum-mapping check-ins reference coherence, not just accuracy.** The skill asks the teacher "does this mapping look right?" but the check is framed around avoiding "curriculum misalignment compounding across many lessons" — accurate, but narrower than ACARA's sequencing guidance, which is as much about logical build-up of sophistication as about correct standard-to-lesson matching. Consider broadening the check-in prompt to ask whether the *progression* (not just the standard-to-lesson mapping) looks right.

## Caveats / contested evidence
Backward design (Wiggins & McTighe) is widely adopted in Australian and international curriculum practice but is a design framework built on professional consensus and wide classroom uptake rather than a directly randomised-trial-tested intervention — its evidentiary status is closer to "well-established professional practice" than "experimentally proven effect on learning outcomes," which is a different kind of evidence than, say, cognitive load theory's controlled studies. The skill is consistent with this status: it treats UbD as one of several optional pedagogy frameworks to apply only if it suits the unit, rather than asserting it as the single correct structure — which is the appropriate level of confidence given the evidence.
