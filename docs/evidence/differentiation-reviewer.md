# Differentiation Reviewer — Evidence Base

**Skill file:** `plugins/skills/differentiation/reviewer/SKILL.md`
**Category:** Differentiation

## What the skill does
Audits an existing lesson plan or unit against five dimensions — universal design, students requiring support, students requiring extension, English language learners (EAL/D/EAL/ELL), and specific learning needs — and returns specific, prioritised, non-judgmental adjustments rather than a rewrite. It offers Universal Design for Learning (UDL) as an optional additional lens once the base review is delivered.

## Evidence base

### Australian Professional Standards for Teachers — Standard 1.5 and "In the Classroom: Differentiation"
- **Publisher/author:** AITSL (Australian Institute for Teaching and School Leadership)
- **Year:** Standards published 2011, revised 2018; illustration-of-practice resource page current
- **URL:** https://www.aitsl.edu.au/teach/improve-practice/in-the-classroom/differentiation
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Standard 1.5 requires all Australian teachers to "differentiate teaching to meet the specific learning needs of students across the full range of abilities"; this AITSL practice page illustrates differentiation as adjusting the content, process, product and learning environment in response to student readiness, interests and learning profile, escalating in sophistication from Graduate through Lead career stages.
- **Relevance to this skill:** This is the direct professional obligation the whole skill operationalises — the reviewer's five-dimension audit (universal design, support, extension, EAL/D, specific needs) is effectively a structured check against Standard 1.5, and AITSL's content/process/product/environment framing maps closely onto how the skill instructs suggestions to be phrased ("where it goes in the lesson, and who it serves").

### Nationally Consistent Collection of Data on School Students with Disability (NCCD): Policy
- **Publisher/author:** Department of Education, Victoria (describing the national NCCD framework, administered jointly by all Australian education authorities)
- **Year:** Framework operating nationally since 2015; funding-linked since 2018 (page reflects current policy)
- **URL:** https://www2.education.vic.gov.au/pal/nccd-students-with-disability/policy
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** All Australian schools must annually identify students receiving a "reasonable adjustment" that lets them access education on the same basis as peers, record the adjustment level (including "quality differentiated teaching" as the base tier), and can draw on existing evidence — IEPs, Student Support Group notes, teacher records — rather than creating new documentation.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Backs the "students requiring support" and "specific learning needs" dimensions of the review — it establishes that "quality differentiated teaching" is itself a recognised, evidenced tier of adjustment under Australian policy, reinforcing the skill's framing of differentiation as an expected professional practice rather than a bonus.

### Students with disability
- **Publisher/author:** ACARA (Australian Curriculum, Assessment and Reporting Authority)
- **Year:** 2022 (Australian Curriculum Version 9)
- **URL:** https://www.australiancurriculum.edu.au/student-diversity/students-with-disability
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Reasonable adjustments should individually adjust the content, process, products and learning environment in response to student goals, abilities and interests, informed by ongoing formative assessment and consultation with the student and family — not a one-size-fits-all approach.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Directly underwrites the "students requiring support" and "specific learning needs" review dimensions, and the skill's principle that suggestions must be specific to where they sit in the lesson and who they serve rather than generic ("provide scaffolding").

### The UDL Guidelines
- **Publisher/author:** CAST (Center for Applied Special Technology)
- **Year:** Version 3.0, 2024
- **URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/
- **Australian source:** No — international framework, offered by AITSL and widely used in Australian schools as a lens, not a mandated Australian standard
- **What it says:** UDL organises design around three principles — multiple means of Engagement, Representation, and Action & Expression — so that flexibility for diverse learners is built into a task from the outset rather than retrofitted for some students.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Directly backs Step 4's "Framework offer," where the skill proposes UDL as a lens for re-examining the plan's underlying flexibility rather than as add-ons — this is precisely CAST's own framing of what UDL is for.

## How this evidence should sharpen the skill
1. **Anchor Step 2's "Universal design" dimension explicitly to Standard 1.5 language.** Currently the reviewer's opening dimension ("does the plan work for most learners as written?") is close to UDL's spirit but doesn't name it; adding a one-line note that this dimension previews the UDL lens offered later in Step 4 would connect the audit structure to the framework it eventually offers, making the offer feel earned rather than bolted on.
2. **Name "quality differentiated teaching" as the floor, not the ceiling, when writing gaps.** The NCCD policy explicitly treats differentiated teaching as the base tier of adjustment before supplementary/substantial/extensive support. The skill's "Specific learning needs" section (Step 2, point 5) could add a line clarifying that where a student is on an NCCD adjustment level, the review should check whether the plan's differentiation is *documented enough* to count as evidence, not just present.
3. **Add content/process/product/environment as explicit review sub-headers.** ACARA and AITSL both use this four-part frame. Restructuring Step 2's "Students requiring support" and "Students requiring extension" bullets under these four categories (rather than the current free-form bullet list) would make the review's structure traceable to a recognised model and easier for a teacher to audit against their own plan later.
4. **Soften the framework offer's UDL description to match CAST 3.0's actual emphasis.** The current offer text (Step 4) describes UDL only as being about flexibility "from the ground up." CAST 3.0 (2024) shifted emphasis toward learner agency and removing bias-driven barriers, not just flexible design — the offer could briefly reflect that update so teachers aren't given a slightly dated picture of the framework if they say yes.

## Caveats / contested evidence
None identified as contested — differentiation as good practice is uncontroversial and mandated under Australian teaching standards and disability law. The main caveat is one of emphasis, not disagreement: NCCD and ACARA both stress that adjustments must be *individually* determined through consultation and assessment data, which a plan-level review (without knowledge of individual students) cannot fully replicate. The skill already handles this reasonably by asking in Step 1 whether there are "specific students or learning needs" to focus on — but it's worth the review output stating plainly, where no specific students were named, that gaps are identified at the level of "a student needing support" in the abstract, not a diagnosis of any real student's needs.
