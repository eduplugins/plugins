# Exit Ticket / Quick Check Creator — Evidence Base

**Skill file:** `plugins/skills/assessment/exit-ticket/SKILL.md`
**Category:** Assessment

## What the skill does
Generates a short, end-of-lesson formative check (written, multiple choice, discussion, or self-assessment format, or a combination) tied directly to the lesson's learning intention rather than the unit as a whole. It gives teachers a triage rule for using the responses ("got it / nearly there / not yet") and a threshold for deciding whether to re-teach, and offers a differentiated version that reduces language demand while keeping the same learning intention.

## Evidence base

### What Works Best 2025 practical guide — Effective feedback
- **Publisher/author:** NSW Department of Education, Centre for Education Statistics and Evaluation (CESE)
- **Year:** 2025 (December)
- **URL:** https://education.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/main-education/about-us/educational-data/cese/What_Works_Best_2025_Effective_feedback_practical_guide.pdf
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Defines effective feedback as "timely, actionable and specific information provided to students that aims to communicate the gap between current and desired performance or understanding, as well as how to close this gap," and states it "should align with the learning intention and success criteria of a task."
- **Relevance to this skill:** This is close to a direct match for the skill's own design brief — exit tickets are explicitly described as "specific," "quick," and "actionable," and every question must tie to the lesson's learning intention. The guide gives that design choice an official, current (2025) evidentiary basis.

### High-impact formative assessment practice
- **Publisher/author:** NSW Department of Education
- **Year:** Undated (current), part of the Department's "Strengthening assessment" hub
- **URL:** https://education.nsw.gov.au/teaching-and-learning/assessment/strengthening-assessment/effective-assessment-practice/high-impact-formative-assessment-practice
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Identifies five high-impact formative assessment strategies — learning intentions and success criteria, effective questioning, feedback, peer/self-assessment, and goal setting — and states assessment is most effective "when it is an integral part of teaching and learning programs."
- **Relevance to this skill:** Backs the skill's self-assessment format (confidence rating + one-sentence justification) and its insistence that the exit ticket be anchored to the lesson's learning intention rather than run as a generic quiz.

### Formative assessment in the classroom (Illustration of Practice)
- **Publisher/author:** Australian Institute for Teaching and School Leadership (AITSL)
- **Year:** Current resource (undated)
- **URL:** https://www.aitsl.edu.au/tools-resources/resource/formative-assessment-in-the-classroom-illustration-of-practice
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Shows teachers combining informal and formal formative assessment methods to check understanding mid-sequence and adjust teaching in response, explicitly linked to Australian Professional Standards for Teachers 4.1 (support student participation) and 5.1 (assess student learning).
- **Relevance to this skill:** Supports the skill's premise that a quick, low-stakes check exists to inform the *next* teaching decision, not just to grade — matching the skill's Step 4 instruction to use exit-ticket results to decide whether to re-teach before moving on.

### Black, P. & Wiliam, D. (1998) — Inside the Black Box: Raising Standards Through Classroom Assessment
- **Publisher/author:** Paul Black and Dylan Wiliam, King's College London (published in Phi Delta Kappan)
- **Year:** 1998
- **URL:** http://allianceforlearning.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2017/03/William-and-Black-Inside-the-Black-Box.pdf
- **Australian source:** No — international (UK), but foundational and among the most-cited formative assessment research worldwide
- **What it says:** A synthesis of ~250 studies finding that improving formative assessment produces effect sizes of 0.4–0.7 on learning gains — larger than most educational interventions — and that self-assessment is reliable ("pupils are generally honest and reliable in assessing... themselves and one another") provided students have "a sufficiently clear picture of the targets that their learning is meant to attain."
- **Relevance to this skill:** Directly underwrites two specific design choices: (1) using a quick, low-stakes formative check at all, given the scale of the effect sizes reported, and (2) including a self-assessment format, since the paper's central caveat on self-assessment reliability (students need a clear target) is exactly why the skill ties every question to the stated learning intention.

## How this evidence should sharpen the skill
1. **Add an explicit "why exit tickets work" line.** The skill currently asserts exit tickets are "quick... and actionable" without evidentiary grounding. A one-line citation (e.g. "formative checks of this kind are linked to some of the largest average effect sizes in education research — Black & Wiliam, 1998") would let teachers justify the practice to colleagues or in professional-learning contexts.
2. **Strengthen the self-assessment format's design note.** Black & Wiliam's finding that self-assessment fails specifically when students lack a clear target suggests the skill's self-assessment prompt ("How confident are you that you can [restate learning intention]?") should be flagged as the *load-bearing* design choice — the confidence rating only works because it restates the learning intention in student language. Consider adding a one-line rationale in Step 3 next to the self-assessment format.
3. **Reference the NSW "success criteria" pairing.** Both NSW DoE sources tie effective feedback/assessment to learning intentions *and* success criteria, not learning intentions alone. The skill's Step 1 only asks for the learning intention. Consider prompting for success criteria too when available, since that's what the cited guides treat as the complete pairing.
4. **Tighten the "not yet" threshold.** Step 4's "if more than a third of the class is 'not yet', consider re-teaching" is a reasonable classroom heuristic but has no cited source — none of the sources found specify a numeric threshold. Either soften this to "consider re-teaching" without the fraction, or explicitly flag it in the skill as a practical rule of thumb rather than a research finding, so teachers don't mistake it for an evidence-based cutoff.

## Caveats / contested evidence
The 0.4–0.7 effect-size range from Black & Wiliam (1998) is widely cited but has also been widely critiqued in the measurement literature (e.g. concerns about how the underlying studies were selected and how effect sizes were aggregated across very different formative assessment interventions). Later meta-analyses (e.g. Hattie, 2009; Kingston & Nash, 2011) report smaller, more variable effect sizes for "formative assessment" as a category. The skill should be read as applying the general *direction* of this evidence (quick, targeted checks that inform next steps are valuable) rather than claiming the specific effect-size figure applies to any single exit ticket.
