# Assessment Schedule — Evidence Base

**Skill file:** `plugins/skills/assessment/schedule/SKILL.md`
**Category:** Assessment

## What the skill does
Builds a term- or year-long assessment schedule showing when each task occurs, its mode, and which curriculum standards it addresses, fetched and suggested (not invented) from live curriculum content and confirmed by the teacher before finalising. It then flags balance issues — assessment clusters, lack of mode variety, and curriculum standards with no assessment coverage.

## Evidence base

### What Works Best 2025 practical guide — Assessment
- **Publisher/author:** NSW Department of Education, Centre for Education Statistics and Evaluation (CESE)
- **Year:** 2025 (December)
- **URL:** https://education.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/main-education/about-us/educational-data/cese/What_Works_Best_2025_Assessment_practical_guide.pdf
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** States that "a carefully considered combination of formative and summative assessment tasks can significantly impact learning culture," and that assessment tasks "provide teachers with invaluable information to assist students in identifying and overcoming barriers to their learning." It also recommends teachers collaboratively plan and moderate assessments across a program, not just design individual tasks in isolation.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Directly supports the skill's balance-review step (Step 4), which checks for over-concentration of summative tasks and insufficient mode variety — this guide is the clearest Australian source for the claim that the *mix* of formative and summative tasks across a program, not just individual task quality, matters for student learning.

### Purpose of assessment
- **Publisher/author:** NSW Education Standards Authority (NESA)
- **Year:** Current (live curriculum reference page)
- **URL:** https://curriculum.nsw.edu.au/assessment-and-reporting/assessment-in-stage-6/purpose-of-assessment
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Distinguishes assessment *for* learning (feedback on strengths/improvement areas), *as* learning (students monitoring their own progress), and *of* learning (evidence and reporting of achievement against syllabus outcomes), and notes summative marks should "provide appropriate discrimination between students" for standards-referenced reporting.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Grounds the schedule template's "Type" column (Formative/Summative) and its "Reporting alignment" section, which explicitly separates which tasks "feed into end-of-term or end-of-year reporting" — this mirrors NESA's for/of-learning distinction almost exactly.

### Planning, teaching, assessing and reporting
- **Publisher/author:** Australian Curriculum, Assessment and Reporting Authority (ACARA)
- **Year:** Current (V9 Australian Curriculum help page)
- **URL:** https://www.australiancurriculum.edu.au/help/f-10-curriculum-overview/planning--teaching--assessing-and-reporting
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Achievement standards describe the quality of learning expected by the end of a year level/band and act as "the clearest starting point for curriculum planning, assessment, and reporting" across a whole teaching program, not just a single task.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Supports the skill's requirement to map every scheduled task to specific curriculum standards, and its balance check that flags "standards with no assessment task" — treating standards coverage across the whole schedule, not just per-task alignment, as the unit of planning is consistent with ACARA's framing of achievement standards as the program-level anchor.

### Australian Professional Standards for Teachers — Standard 5.1
- **Publisher/author:** Australian Institute for Teaching and School Leadership (AITSL)
- **Year:** Current standards (live reference document)
- **URL:** https://www.aitsl.edu.au/standards
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Standard 5.1 requires teachers to "Demonstrate understanding of assessment strategies, including informal and formal, diagnostic, formative and summative approaches to assess student learning."
- **Relevance to this skill:** Supports the skill's Step 1 planning question about mode variety (written/oral/multimodal/practical/digital) and its balance check flagging "all tasks in the same mode" — a schedule with only one assessment type doesn't demonstrate the range of strategies AITSL expects teachers to be able to deploy.

## How this evidence should sharpen the skill
1. **Give the balance-check thresholds an evidentiary caveat.** Step 4's rule ("More than 2 summative tasks in consecutive weeks → suggest redistributing") is a sensible workload/wellbeing heuristic but isn't drawn from any of the sources found — none specify a numeric threshold for task clustering. Consider labelling these as practical scheduling heuristics rather than implying they are evidence-based cutoffs, similar to the caveat recommended for Exit Ticket's "one-third of the class" rule.
2. **Add an "as learning" checkpoint to the schedule template.** NESA's three-part purpose framing (for/as/of learning) suggests a well-rounded schedule should include not just formative and summative tasks but also points where students self-monitor progress (assessment *as* learning). The current template's Type column only has Formative/Summative. Consider adding a note or optional tag for tasks that build in structured self-assessment or reflection, consistent with NESA's third category.
3. **Strengthen the "Standards coverage" balance check with the ACARA framing.** Currently Step 4 flags standards gaps but the skill doesn't explain *why* full-program standards coverage matters. A short line citing ACARA's framing of achievement standards as the year-level reference point for reporting would help teachers understand that a coverage gap isn't just an administrative oversight — it risks leaving parts of the reported achievement standard unassessed by year's end.
4. **Cross-reference the collaborative/moderation angle from the NSW Assessment guide.** That guide recommends collaboratively planning and moderating assessment across a program, not just per-task. The skill currently treats the schedule as a single-teacher planning exercise. Consider adding an optional next-step prompt (similar to other skills' handoff offers) suggesting the teacher share the finalised schedule with their faculty/team for moderation input, particularly where multiple teachers deliver the same year level.

## Caveats / contested evidence
None of the sources found specify an ideal or evidence-based ratio of formative to summative tasks, or an optimal spacing between summative assessments — the "balance" language in both the skill and the NSW DoE guide is directional (avoid clustering, avoid mode monotony) rather than backed by a specific quantitative target. The skill's balance-check rules should therefore continue to be framed as practical scheduling heuristics informed by this general direction in the evidence, not as precise, evidence-derived numbers.
