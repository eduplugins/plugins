# Report Comment Writer — Evidence Base

**Skill file:** `plugins/skills/communication/report-comment/SKILL.md`
**Category:** Communication

## What the skill does
Drafts individualised student report comments grounded in real, fetched achievement standards language, using teacher-supplied achievement judgments and observations (never inferring a grade), varying comment structure per student, and producing drafts explicitly flagged for teacher review rather than finished text.

## Evidence base

### Reporting advice — Writing report comments
- **Publisher/author:** NSW Department of Education
- **Year:** Current (page last modified 2026)
- **URL:** https://education.nsw.gov.au/teaching-and-learning/curriculum/reporting-to-parents/report-comments
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** States that report comments must "include personalised, constructive statements," warns against uncritical use of comment banks (comments "must reflect informed, individualised professional judgement"), and requires at least one area for improvement stated using actionable, constructive language.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Near-direct match to the skill's own rules — "vary the openings," "never write the same comment structure for every student," and the requirement for a forward-looking growth area in every comment (Step 5) mirror this policy almost exactly.

### Reporting Student Achievement and Progress F–10: Efficient reporting practices and model reports
- **Publisher/author:** Victorian Department of Education
- **Year:** Current (Policy and Advisory Library)
- **URL:** https://www2.education.vic.gov.au/pal/reporting-student-achievement/guidance/efficient-reporting-practices-and-model-reports
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Recommends short summary comments (around 100 words) in primary reports and explicitly discourages both lengthy detailed comments and the practice of maintaining static comment banks in semester reports.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Supports the skill's default 60–80 word length and its "never write the same comment structure for every student" rule, while also showing that Australian jurisdictions differ on exactly how much comment text is wanted — relevant to the skill's "match school's word count limit" instruction.

### Australian Professional Standards for Teachers — Focus Area 5.5 (Report on student achievement)
- **Publisher/author:** AITSL
- **Year:** Current standard
- **URL:** https://www.aitsl.edu.au/standards
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Requires teachers to "report clearly, accurately and respectfully to students and parents/carers about student achievement, making use of accurate and reliable records" at the Proficient career stage.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Underpins the skill's core design decision that the teacher — not the AI — supplies the achievement judgment and observations, since the professional standard places the reporting obligation, and the underlying evidentiary record, on the teacher.

### Planning, teaching, assessing and reporting
- **Publisher/author:** ACARA (Australian Curriculum, Assessment and Reporting Authority)
- **Year:** Current (V9 Australian Curriculum)
- **URL:** https://www.australiancurriculum.edu.au/help/f-10-curriculum-overview/planning--teaching--assessing-and-reporting
- **Australian source:** Yes
- **What it says:** Confirms that achievement standards are intended as "a common reference point for reporting to parents/carers" supporting national consistency, while leaving exact report style and format to state/territory authorities and schools.
- **Relevance to this skill:** Directly supports Step 2's requirement to fetch and ground comment language in real achievement standards rather than training-data paraphrase, and the instruction that comment language should be "adapted, not copied verbatim" from the standard.

## How this evidence should sharpen the skill
1. **Flag comment-bank risk explicitly as evidence-based, not stylistic.** The "Never… write the same comment structure for every student" rule in Step 5 could cite that this mirrors explicit NSW DoE and Victorian DoE guidance against static/uncritical comment banks — this would help a teacher understand the rule isn't just good writing practice but matches what their own department may audit against.
2. **Surface jurisdictional length variation as a prompt, not just a default.** Step 4 asks for "word count limit (if the school has one)" and defaults to 60–80 words — given Victoria's guidance explicitly recommends ~100 words and *no* comments at all in secondary reports, the skill could add a short note reminding teachers to check their sector's own policy rather than assuming the 60–80 default is universally appropriate, especially for secondary/VIC contexts.
3. **Anchor "achievement standard grounding" language to the AITSL/ACARA reporting purpose.** Step 5's per-grade-band guidance (A/Excellent through E/Limited) could explicitly note that Step 2's standards-fetch exists because ACARA achievement standards are the designated "common reference point" for reporting — this justifies the never-skip Step 2 requirement.
4. **Consider adding an explicit comment-bank caution to the Never list.** Step 5's "Never" list (no achievement-grade leakage, no "try harder" phrasing, no invented details, no repeated structure) could add a line: never let a generated comment be reused verbatim for another student without teacher-confirmed changes — directly operationalising the NSW/VIC comment-bank warnings for an AI-drafting context, where the underlying risk (near-identical output) is easier to fall into than with manual writing.

## Caveats / contested evidence
Victorian guidance actively *discourages* long personalised written comments and comment banks in favour of brevity and rating-based reporting, while NSW guidance requires personalised written comments with specific improvement areas — the skill's approach (individualised, ~60–80 word comments) sits closer to the NSW model. Schools in Victoria or other jurisdictions with brief/ratings-based reporting formats may find the skill's default comment length and structure a mismatch for local policy, so the "match school's word count limit" instruction in Step 4 is doing real work and should not be treated as a minor formality.
